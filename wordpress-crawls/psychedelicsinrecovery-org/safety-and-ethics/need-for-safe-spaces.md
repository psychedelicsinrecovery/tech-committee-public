## Safe Spaces

A safe space allows members to explore their experiences with psychedelics, discuss their motivations, and assess the impact on their recovery journey without the threat of being ostracized or judged.

Psychedelics, including substances like psilocybin, MDMA, and LSD, are being studied and used in controlled settings to address various mental health issues. For some individuals in recovery, psychedelics have provided profound healing experiences that traditional abstinence-based approaches may not fully address. These experiences can range from alleviating severe depression and anxiety to gaining deep insights into one's behaviors and traumas, which can be integral to long-term recovery.

However, discussing the use of psychedelics in traditional recovery meetings can be fraught with challenges. Abstinence-based programs often view the use of any mind-altering substance as a relapse, creating a conflict for individuals who may benefit from psychedelic therapy but fear judgment or exclusion from their recovery community. This conflict underscores the necessity of having dedicated spaces where these conversations can occur openly and safely.

#### The Need for Safe Spaces

1. **Safety**: The primary concern in creating a space for discussing psychedelic use in recovery is safety. Individuals need to feel secure in sharing their experiences without fear of repercussions. This safety includes not just physical and emotional protection but also confidentiality, ensuring that what is shared in the group remains within the group.
2. **Vulnerability**: Authentic conversations require vulnerability. For many, discussing psychedelic use in the context of recovery can be daunting due to the stigma associated with drug use. A safe space encourages individuals to be open about their experiences, fears, and the complexities of their recovery.
3. **Protection**: Protection in this context refers to safeguarding individuals from harm, both within and outside the group. This includes protection from judgment, misunderstanding, and the potential misuse of psychedelics. Establishing clear guidelines for the group is crucial.
4. **Freedom from Judgment**: A non-judgmental space is essential for individuals to explore the intersection of psychedelics and recovery. Judgment can lead to shame and isolation, which are counterproductive to recovery.
5. **Ability to Report Negative Behaviors**: A safe space must allow members to report negative behaviors or experiences associated with psychedelic use without fear of retribution.

As the role of psychedelics in mental health and addiction recovery continues to evolve, it is crucial to provide safe spaces for individuals in traditional abstinence-based recovery programs to discuss their experiences — prioritizing safety, vulnerability, protection, freedom from judgment, and the ability to report negative behaviors.
