## FAQ

Community generated frequently asked questions. [View the PDF](https://www.psychedelicsinrecovery.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/PIR_FAQ_2026.pdf)

**Who are we, and why are we here?**
Psychedelics in Recovery is a 12-step fellowship of people from many 12-step programs and other paths of recovery who share our experience, strength, and hope with each other. Our primary purpose is to pursue recovery, as defined by the individual, and to help others do the same. The only requirement for membership is a desire to recover from addiction and/or an interest in the use of psychedelics and/or plant medicines as an aid to our process of recovery.

**Is this Fellowship for people who are addicted to psychedelics?**
No. PIR is focused on integrating the safe, intentional, and/or ceremonial use of psychedelics and/or plant medicines into a healthy 12-step process of recovery.

**How does PIR help?**
PIR is open to all seeking recovery from addiction, with awareness of the potential for responsible psychedelic/plant medicine use to improve individual and communal lives. It's an informal, peer support space with a spectrum of group-sharing models, from silent acceptance to kind reflection ("feedback").

**When and how do I ask questions?**
Best asked of experienced members in the virtual "parking lot" after the meeting — the "meeting after the meeting." We share experience but don't give advice. The parking lot is not a space to self-promote for financial or material gain.

**How are PIR meetings similar or different from other 12-Step fellowship meetings?**
More accommodating to a wider range of recovery approaches, including harm-reduction and medication-assisted therapy, while opening with standard readings (adapted 12 Steps) and sharing. Psychedelic/plant medicine use is not stigmatized; members self-define recovery/sobriety/abstinence.

**Are people in PIR in other recovery programs, too?**
Many are also in AA, NA, ACA, Al-Anon, CoDA, Recovery Dharma, and non-faith-based fellowships. Some struggle with substances; others with process/behavioral addictions like gambling, overeating, or codependency.

**Do I have to work the steps to recover?**
No — members find their own path; working the Steps is encouraged, not required.

**Do I have to admit I have a problem with drugs or alcohol?**
Admission of powerlessness over addiction is the First Step; members are encouraged toward rigorous honesty, sharing when comfortable and safe.

**What does sobriety mean in PIR?**
Members define it for themselves — abstinence from self-defined harmful behaviors, substance- or process-based (alcohol, narcotics, tobacco, codependence, self-injury, gambling, food, etc.).

**What about craving or relapse when using psychedelics/plant medicines?**
Craving and relapse are addiction phenomena; most psychedelics are not considered inherently addictive. If craving/relapse occurs, members contact sponsors or trusted others to restore healthy recovery. See the Cares and Considerations document (safety-and-ethics page).

**How do I identify my sobriety in PIR?**
Members self-define sobriety/clean time, find sponsors or co-travelers, work steps toward connection with a Higher Power, and take service commitments.

**Am I still "sober" if I use psychedelics?**
Each member defines this for themselves. PIR sees these substances as medicine, used consciously and integrated into 12-step recovery — that integration process is central to PIR. PIR does not judge anyone's psychedelic use or integration choices.

**What is considered destructive or chaotic use of psychedelics?**
Using to escape feelings or avoid life circumstances; misuse impacting physical/mental health or relationships. Rigorous honesty with self and others is encouraged to stay accountable.

**How can you tell the difference between "integrating" and "using"?**
Integration is what happens before/during/after psychedelic or ceremonial work, individually or in community. "Using" carries a negative connotation of ingesting substances or engaging in harmful behaviors against one's recovery.

**How does a newcomer get started integrating psychedelics/plant medicines into their recovery?**
Most start by attending meetings, asking others how they incorporated psychedelics into 12-step work, researching from reputable sources, and proceeding with caution guided by Higher Power and trusted others.

**Where's the line between therapeutic use & abuse?**
Each member defines this for themselves; PIR members refrain from judging others' paths and share from their own experience, strength, and hope (ESH).

**How do you know when you're relapsing or have lost your sobriety?**
Many find it helpful to define personal "Bottom Line Behaviors" (BLBs) — substances or process-oriented behaviors (porn, gambling, self-injury) they choose not to engage in.

**How do I make friends and build community within PIR?**
Attend meetings and parking lots, take service commitments, join unofficial social media platforms, and respectfully exchange (and use) phone numbers.

**I'm in another fellowship and have taken psychedelics/plant medicine — do I have to restart my sobriety there?**
Sharing about psychedelics is often deemed an "outside issue" in mainstream 12-step meetings. PIR does NOT consider intentional psychedelic use/integration a relapse or slip; each member chooses for themselves whether to restart clean time in their other fellowship.

**Does PIR promote drug use?**
No. PIR doesn't consider psychedelics "drugs" when used intentionally for healing, and affirms their potential for physical, spiritual, mental, and emotional healing — but each member decides for themselves. *Note: PIR prohibits promotion — sourcing medicines or recommending individuals/retreat centers breaks Traditions and conflicts with the primary purpose.*
