## Safety and Ethics

As PIR grows, we need to establish a safe container for all of us to recover together. We can be willful people and many of us come here with a history of trauma.

We suggest each group and individual member maintain an atmosphere of recovery. Be mindful of language, screen etiquette, crosstalk, sexual or inappropriate behavior in the meeting or in the "parking lot" after the meeting.

### Overview

This document is offered as a resource to assist with the productive use of psychedelics in any recovery process, including 12-step-based recovery. It is also meant to raise awareness around how psychedelic medicines are used and the ways such use may impact individuals and communities in the areas of physical, mental, social, cultural, emotional, and spiritual health and healing. Psychedelics in Recovery does not promote the use of psychedelics.

### For Those New to Psychedelics

First-time users may choose to approach these substances with caution. Reading about psychedelic experiences (e.g. www.erowid.org), conversations with trusted friends, and preparation resources (e.g. tripsitters.org) are good starting points. See the "Resources" section below.

### CARES

**Set & Setting** — "Set and setting matter." Set is one's state of mind, heart and soul when intending to use psychedelics. Setting is the geography, environment, objects, music, and beings that make up the physical space. Both are vital components of preparation and integration.

**Safety & Substance** — Safety (physical, emotional, psychological, social, spiritual) is the highest priority. Substance means knowing the source, purity, strength and suggested dose of what one is considering ingesting.

**Structure & Support** — Structure is the system a person builds for preparation, the journey itself, and integration (journaling, meditation, diet, exercise, social connection). Support is core to 12-step recovery: work with a guide, coach, therapist, shaman, or trusted fellow member; PIR is not a substitute for medical advice.

**Spirituality & Sustainability** — Psychedelic experience and recovery share a spiritual basis, but with shared vulnerability to "spiritual bypassing" — using spirituality to avoid facing unresolved emotional issues. Humility is the foundation of any recovery process; grandiosity is the greatest threat to it.

### CONSIDERATIONS

**Endangered Sources** — We encourage respect for the cultural/historical lineages of these medicines and awareness that sources like peyote and Bufo Alvarius (Sonoran Desert Toad) venom are endangered; synthetic 5-MEO-DMT and San Pedro are noted as sustainable alternatives some members use.

**Legal Status** — These substances remain illegal in most of the US and world; there can be legal ramifications for sitters/guides, especially if someone is injured under their care.

**Reminder of Risks and Research** — PIR aspires not to glorify psychedelics as a "magic pill." "Doing the work" includes 12-step work with a sponsor, recovery coaching, meditation, healthy habits, meetings, goal setting, exercise, journaling. Some members have misused these substances; misuse can lead to unhealthy habits or dependence requiring treatment/abstinence. Members are urged to research contraindications with medications/conditions and consult a licensed practitioner, and not to wean off psychiatric medication without medical guidance.

### Protecting PIR as a Whole / Safety in Meetings

Legality: psychedelic medicines are not legal/decriminalized in many places — PIR does not promote psychedelic services or disclose where to find substances in meetings or "parking lots."

Each meeting is autonomous, guided by group conscience, with the primary purpose to pursue recovery and welcome newcomers. Suggested format: adapted 12-Steps, Integration, Plan of Action, Guiding Principles. Inappropriate behavior (bullying, derogatory comments, medical/psychiatric advice, self-promotion, predatory comments) is discouraged. Each meeting is encouraged to have a General Service Representative (GSR) connecting it to PIR as a whole; GSRs attend a monthly GSR Business Meeting.
